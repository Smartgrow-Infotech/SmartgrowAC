What it is
### How whole-home dehumidifiers work.
A standalone dehumidifier (about the size of a mid-tower PC) gets installed on your HVAC ductwork or in a problem area like a basement. It runs independent of the AC — pulls air through a refrigerant coil, condenses moisture out, drains it to a floor drain or condensate pump.
Unlike portable dehumidifiers (which need emptying daily and only cover one room), whole-home units handle the entire house, distribute through HVAC, and never need attention beyond an annual filter change. Why dehumidifiers in Hudson County
### Summer here is humid, and AC alone isn’t always enough.
Hudson County summer humidity averages 65–80% outdoors. Even with AC running, indoor humidity often sits at 55–65% — high enough for mold, mustiness, and sticky discomfort. ### Prevents mold growth
Mold thrives above 60% humidity. Basements, crawl spaces, and closets in older buildings often run that high in summer despite AC. Dehumidifiers keep humidity in the 45–55% range where mold can’t reproduce. ### Solves the “muggy” feeling
AC removes some humidity as a side effect of cooling — but in modern over-sized systems, the AC short-cycles before dehumidifying enough. A dedicated dehumidifier handles humidity separately so the AC just cools. ### Protects basements & finishes
Hoboken brownstone basements (and JC waterfront condos) are notorious for summer dampness. Wood furniture, books, electronics, drywall — all degrade in chronic high humidity. A dehumidifier protects all of it.

Sizing & options
### Picking the right dehumidifier.
Whole-home dehumidifier sizing is measured in pints per day. Three or four decisions drive the spec.
01
### Pints/day capacity
70 PPD: small homes (up to ~2,000 sqft) and basements. 90–100 PPD: average homes. 120+ PPD: large homes or extreme humidity situations. Oversizing is fine; undersizing means constant running.
02
### Ducted vs. standalone
Ducted (integrated with HVAC) covers the whole home, easy to install during AC season. Standalone (placed in basement or problem area) targets one space, needs only a power outlet and a drain — cheaper, faster.
03
### Dehumidistat & controls
Most units have built-in humidistats. Some integrate with the HVAC thermostat (Ecobee, Honeywell). Smart integration prevents the AC and dehumidifier from fighting each other.
04
### Drain options
Gravity drain to a floor drain: free. Condensate pump: $150–$300 if no drain available. Some units have built-in pumps. We’ll plan during the in-home estimate.
What installation involves
Most dehumidifier installs are clean, half-day jobs. Two configurations drive scope.
01
### Integration with HVAC
Ducted install: cuts into return air, integrates with the HVAC system, distributes dehumidified air everywhere. Standalone install in basement: just needs a power outlet and a drain.
02
### Drain routing
Gravity drain to nearest floor drain is simplest. If no drain is available, we add a small condensate pump. Sometimes drain lines need to be run a fair distance — we’ll quote based on layout.
03
### Smart-control integration
Optional — some thermostats can control the dehumidifier directly, preventing conflict with AC. Ecobee, Honeywell smart thermostats handle this; older thermostats don’t.

### Common questions about dehumidifiers.
Doesn’t my AC dehumidify? Some — cooling air naturally condenses moisture out of it. But modern oversized AC systems short-cycle before they dehumidify adequately. Result: cool but clammy indoor air. A separate dehumidifier handles humidity decoupled from cooling, so the AC can run for cooling only.
What humidity level should I target? 45–55% relative humidity in summer. Below 40% feels dry; above 60% feels muggy and risks mold. Most dehumidifiers default to 50% — works well for most Hudson County homes.
Whole-home vs. portable — which? If the issue is one room (a damp basement, a single closet), a quality portable dehumidifier might be enough. If the issue is whole-home (general mugginess, multiple damp areas, mustiness throughout), whole-home wins by a lot — covers everything, never needs emptying.
How big a unit do I need? Rule of thumb: 0.04–0.05 pints per day per sqft. A 2,500 sqft home needs ~100–125 PPD. Basements alone usually need a 70–90 PPD unit. We measure on the in-home estimate.
Will a dehumidifier raise my electric bill a lot? Modest impact. A modern whole-home dehumidifier uses ~600–800 watts when running, similar to a high-end laptop. Annual operating cost: maybe $80–$200 depending on usage. Much less than running a portable in every room.
My basement has standing-water issues. Will a dehumidifier fix it? No — a dehumidifier handles ambient humidity, not water intrusion. If you have actual water coming in (foundation leaks, drainage issues), you need a waterproofing solution first. A dehumidifier complements waterproofing but isn’t a substitute.
