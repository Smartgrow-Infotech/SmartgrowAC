What we see most
### Common heating problems we fix.
If you’re experiencing any of these in your Hudson County home, we’ve seen it hundreds of times. ### Furnace won't ignite
Pilot light, gas valve, ignitor, or control board issue. We diagnose before any work and quote up front. ### Boiler losing pressure
Bad relief valve, expansion tank, or system leak. Common on older NJ boilers and an easy diagnostic visit. ### No heat or blowing cold
Thermostat issue, ductwork problem, failed heat exchanger, or flame sensor. Often a same-day fix. ### Banging or clanging noises
Air in the lines, water hammer in steam boilers, expansion creaks. Often points to a service-needed condition. ### Uneven heat between floors
Zoning, ductwork balancing, or system sizing. Bedrooms colder than the main floor is a common complaint we solve. ### High utility bills
Inefficient burner, leaky ducts, aging system, or wrong-size unit. We’ll show you the math.

### Common questions about heating service.
How often should I service my heating system? Once a year — ideally in early fall before the first cold snap. Annual tune-ups catch cracked heat exchangers, dirty burners, and pressure issues before they become emergency calls. Our Combo Plan ($245/yr) covers fall heating + spring AC tune-ups.
What's the difference between a furnace and a boiler? A furnace heats air and pushes it through ducts. A boiler heats water and circulates it through radiators or radiant floors. Many Hoboken brownstones and older Jersey City row homes have boilers (steam or hot-water). Most newer condos and single-family homes have furnaces with central air. We service both.
Should I repair or replace my old furnace or boiler? Repair if: system is under 12 years old, repair cost is under 30% of replacement, and you’ve kept up with annual maintenance. Replace if: 15+ years old (especially boilers, which can outlast that but often inefficiently), repair cost 50%+ of new, or AFUE rating under 80%. Modern systems run 90-98% AFUE — major utility savings.
Do you service oil heat? Yes — oil-fired furnaces and boilers, plus oil-to-gas and oil-to-heat-pump conversion projects. NJ has been steadily phasing out oil heat, and many Hudson County homes are good candidates for conversion (with utility rebates).
How long do furnaces and boilers last? Furnaces: typically 15-20 years. Cast-iron boilers: often 25-30 years (some original Hoboken brownstone boilers are 50+, though running inefficiently). Heat pumps: 12-15 years. Annual maintenance extends lifespan significantly.
Do you do gas conversions? Yes — oil-to-gas conversions are common in Hudson County, especially for older brownstones. We handle the system replacement, gas line work coordination with PSE&G, and permits. Often pairs with a high-efficiency boiler or furnace install.
