When it’s time to replace
### Signs your AC is past its prime.
Not every AC needs replacing — but some signs are pretty clear. If two or more of these are true, replacement is usually the better call. ### 12+ years old
Most central AC systems run 12–15 years. Ductless and PTAC are similar. Past that, parts get harder to find and efficiency drops sharply. ### Repair cost is 50%+ of new
Once you’re quoting a $4,000 repair on a 14-year-old system, replacing usually pencils out better — with better efficiency and a new warranty. ### R-22 refrigerant
EPA phased out R-22 in 2020. The remaining supply is expensive, and a leak basically forces replacement on older systems. ### Climbing utility bills
Steady usage but rising bills usually means the system is working harder than it should. Often points to compressor wear or refrigerant loss. ### Can’t reach setpoint
If the system runs constantly without cooling the space, either it’s undersized, the ductwork leaks, or it’s near end-of-life. Worth a load calc. ### Multiple breakdowns this year
Two or three repair calls in one summer is usually the system telling you it’s done. Replacement starts to look like the cheaper path.

What affects the price
AC install pricing varies more than repair does. Three things drive most of it.
01
### System type & size
A 2-ton central AC replacement is a different job than a 4-zone ductless install or a high-output PTAC. Manual J load calc tells us what you actually need.
02
### Ductless vs. ducted complexity
Replacing in-place is cheaper than running new line sets through plaster brownstone walls. We’ll quote both routes if there’s a choice.
03
### Building access & permits
Hoboken/JC high-rises often need permits, freight elevator booking, and after-hours condenser placement. We coordinate all of it.

### Common questions about AC installation.
How long does an install take? Most ductless mini-splits: 1–2 days. Central AC replacement (existing ductwork): 1 day. New central AC with new ductwork: 2–4 days. PTAC unit swaps: a few hours per unit. We’ll walk you through the timeline before scheduling.
What’s Manual J and why does it matter? Manual J is the industry-standard load calculation — it accounts for square footage, insulation, windows, sun exposure, ductwork, and climate. Eyeball sizing (“a 3-ton should do it”) is how systems end up oversized or undersized. Right-sized AC saves 15–30% on operating cost and runs quieter.
Do I need permits in Hoboken or Jersey City? Yes for most equipment replacement and all new installations. We pull permits as part of the install — you don’t deal with city hall. For condo buildings we also coordinate with building management for approvals.
Is financing available? Yes — through {LINK:/financing/}NEIF for residential installs. We’ll show you the monthly payment alongside the cash price so you can compare. Commercial work goes through different channels.
Which brand do you recommend? Depends on the application. Fujitsu is our specialty for ductless (brownstones, row homes, condo retrofits). Friedrich for PTAC (Hoboken/JC high-rises). York, Carrier, and Trane for central AC. We’ll explain the trade-offs in plain language.
Can you install ductless in a brownstone without breaking up walls? Yes — this is one of our most common installs. Line sets typically run through closets, behind crown molding, or up exterior walls hidden in line-set covers. Single-zone runs $4,000–$7,000 installed; multi-zone $10,000–$20,000+.
