What it is
### How commercial rooftop HVAC works.
A packaged rooftop unit (RTU) contains the full HVAC system — compressor, condenser, heat exchanger, blower, controls — in a single weatherproof cabinet that sits on a curb on the building’s roof. Ductwork drops through the curb into the occupied space below, delivering conditioned air to supply registers throughout the building.
Most commercial buildings in Hoboken and Jersey City use rooftop units. They’re practical because the ground-level real estate is too valuable to give up to mechanical equipment — and putting it on the roof keeps the noise out of the workspace. Why we own rooftop work

Most HVAC contractors won’t take on rooftop replacements in Hudson County — the logistics are intimidating. That’s our bread and butter. ### Crane coordination
We schedule the crane, coordinate the lift with the building owner, and handle the rigging. Old RTU comes off; new RTU goes on; same day usually. Permitting and crane firm relationships in place. ### Permits & street closures

Sizing & options
### Picking the right RTU.
Commercial RTU sizing has more variables than residential. Get it wrong and you have a $40,000 unit that doesn’t condition the space.
01
### BTU / tonnage per sqft
Commercial rule of thumb: roughly 1 ton per 400–500 sqft, but it varies wildly with occupancy, equipment heat, window load, and ventilation requirements. We do a Manual N (commercial load) calculation.
02
### Single-zone vs. multi-zone
Small spaces (5,000–15,000 sqft): one large RTU often handles the load with internal zoning. Larger spaces: multiple RTUs with dedicated zones. We map this on the site visit.
03
### Gas heat vs. electric heat
Most Hudson County commercial buildings spec gas heat for cost. Electric heat is used for spaces without gas service or for backup. Heat pump RTUs are increasingly common for electrification mandates.
04
### Curb compatibility
If the existing roof curb matches the new RTU, the swap is dramatically faster. New curbs require additional roofing, sealing, and structural verification. We measure the existing curb on the site visit.
What installation involves
Commercial RTU installs are bigger projects than any residential work. Four pieces drive the project.
01
### Crane lift & rigging
Scheduled with a local crane firm, sized to the unit weight and rooftop access. Usually a 3–6 hour window of street activity. We coordinate with the city and (if needed) police escort for traffic management.
02
### Permits & building dept.
Building permits, electrical permits, gas permits (if applicable), and structural review for roof-load compliance. We handle the full permitting package.
03
### Roof curb & weatherproofing
Roof curb compatibility, flashing, weatherproofing, and roofing-warranty coordination with your existing roofer. We’ll bring in a roofing partner if curb work is needed.

### Common questions about commercial rooftop HVAC.
How long do commercial RTUs last? 10–20 years depending on duty cycle and maintenance. Restaurant RTUs (hood replacement air, long runtimes) often need replacement at 8–12 years. Office/retail RTUs commonly run 15–20 years with quarterly maintenance.
How does crane-lift coordination work? We schedule the crane with a local firm sized to the unit and roof access. Most Hoboken/JC sites require a city permit and (depending on street width) police-coordinated traffic management. We handle all of it — you get a date and a 3–6 hour window.
What permits are required? Mechanical permit (always), electrical permit (always for new equipment), gas permit (if applicable), structural review (for roof loading), and street-closure permit for the crane lift. Commercial work typically has more permit requirements than residential.
Maintenance contracts — what’s included? Per-unit quarterly or semi-annual visits: filter changes, belt inspection, refrigerant pressure check, electrical test, condensate cleaning, coil cleaning (as needed). Custom add-ons: hood-exhaust service for restaurants, dampers for office buildings, etc. Per-unit contract pricing scales with the building.
My existing roof curb — will the new RTU fit? Most RTU manufacturers make curb-adapter kits for common older units. We measure your existing curb on the site visit and either match the new RTU to the curb or quote a curb-adapter / new curb installation. About 60% of replacements use the existing curb.
Which brand for commercial? Carrier, Trane, and Lennox are the most common in the area — strong parts supply, local service technicians available across all of them. Coleman/York are also solid for budget-conscious projects. Brand matters less than installer expertise on commercial work.
