Symptoms we diagnose
### Common boiler failure patterns.
Boilers fail differently than furnaces. Old Hoboken steam systems especially have their own dialect — we speak it fluently.
[svg] 
### Won’t fire
Pilot, thermocouple, gas valve, control board, or low-water cutoff. We’ll trace it through the safety chain in order — no skipping steps.
[svg] 
### Pressure too high or low
Hydronic boilers run 12–15 PSI cold, 20–25 hot. Outside that range usually means a bad expansion tank, failed pressure-reducing valve, or air in the system. Steam runs much lower — different troubleshooting.
[svg] 
### Banging radiators (steam)
Almost always pitched piping or failed air vents — not the boiler itself. Old steam systems have specific fixes for this, and we know them well.
[svg] 
### Leak at the base
Condensate from a condensing boiler is normal. Water from the relief valve, expansion tank, or pipe fittings is not. We’ll find the source and quote the fix.
[svg] 
### No hot water (combi units)
Combi boilers heat both your radiators and your domestic hot water. If hot water stops but heat works (or vice versa), it usually points to a diverter valve or flow sensor.
[svg] 
### Pilot won’t stay lit
Thermocouple, drafty pilot tube, or weak gas pressure. Standard order of diagnosis. Usually a same-day fix once we’re on-site.
How a repair visit works
### Diagnose first. Quote next. Then fix.
Same diagnostic discipline across steam, hydronic, and combi boilers — old buildings get extra attention.
01
### Phone triage
We ask what the system’s doing, what brand and age, and whether it’s a true emergency — before we send a truck. Saves you a trip fee on simple fixes.
02
### On-site diagnostic
Full system check — not just the loudest symptom. We isolate the actual cause so the repair fixes the real problem.
03
### Honest quote
Parts, labor, and your options in writing before any work starts. If repair vs. replace is close, we’ll show you both numbers.
04
### Fix & verify
We repair, test the full cycle, and explain what we did. Clean up. Most jobs get a workmanship warranty on top of the parts warranty.
What affects the cost
Boiler repair varies more than furnace repair. The system type and the building age both play in.
01
### Boiler type
Modern combi or condensing boiler — mostly stocked parts, predictable repairs. Old cast-iron hydronic or atmospheric steam boiler — sometimes specialty parts and old fittings.
02
### Age & parts
Pre-2000 boilers in Hoboken brownstones sometimes need parts sourced specially. We’ll tell you on the phone whether it’s a stock part or a longer wait.
03
### Pipe access in old buildings
Old basements with low headroom, painted-over pipe joints, or asbestos insulation all add time. We work safely around them — that’s real cost, not a surprise.

Quick answers
### Common questions about boiler repair.
Steam vs. hydronic — what’s the difference?
 [svg] 
Steam: boiler heats water to steam, which rises through pipes to radiators, condenses, and returns by gravity. Common in pre-1950 Hoboken buildings. Hydronic: boiler heats water, which is pumped through pipes to radiators or radiant floors at much lower pressure. Modern installs are almost all hydronic. We service both.
Why do my radiators bang?
 [svg] 
Almost always one of three things: condensate trapped in the radiator (bad pitch), a failed air vent, or condensate trapped in horizontal piping. Almost never the boiler itself. Cheap to fix once diagnosed, dramatic to live with.
What’s a pressure-relief valve?
 [svg] 
Safety valve that opens if boiler pressure gets too high. If yours is dripping, it’s either doing its job (and pressure is actually too high — needs investigation) or it’s failed open (needs replacement). Don’t cap it — ever.
How long do boilers last?
 [svg] 
Cast-iron hydronic boilers in Hoboken brownstones routinely run 30–40 years with maintenance. Modern condensing boilers: 15–20 years. Steam boilers (atmospheric): 25–30 years. Old doesn’t automatically mean replace.
Old brownstone with steam — can you keep it running?
 [svg] 
Yes — this is a big part of what we do. Steam systems are fixable, parts are still available for most older boilers, and the system is often the best fit for the building. We can also discuss conversion to hydronic if it ever makes sense.
Annual boiler flush — needed?
 [svg] 
Steam boilers: yes — sediment buildup at the bottom can cause overheating and shorten life. Skim work as needed. Hydronic boilers: less critical, but a system flush every 5–10 years is good practice, especially when adding fluid additives.
