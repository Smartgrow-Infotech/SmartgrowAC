Why pre-season tune-ups
### What yearly heating maintenance gets you.
A tune-up isn’t just clean-and-go. For combustion equipment it’s also a safety check — one of the few preventable causes of household CO incidents.
[svg] 
### CO safety inspection
Cracked heat exchangers, blocked flues, and bad seals release carbon monoxide. We use a calibrated combustion analyzer — not a sniff test.
[svg] 
### Prevents January breakdowns
January breakdowns are almost always traceable to issues that were visible the previous fall. Catching them in October beats waiting in line during a cold snap.
[svg] 
### Maintains efficiency
Soot, scale, and dirty burners drag combustion efficiency down by 5–15% over a season. A clean system burns the gas you paid for.
[svg] 
### Keeps your warranty valid
Almost all manufacturer warranties (Carrier, Trane, Navien, Weil-McLain, etc.) require documented annual service. Skip it and a $1,500 control board claim could be denied.
[svg] 
### Cleans soot & scale
Combustion residue and water-side scale accumulate quietly. Annual cleaning prevents the long-term wear that kills heat exchangers.
[svg] 
### Pick your slot
We hold pre-season schedules so you don’t fight November/December demand. Pick a weekday, get the same tech each year.

Real cleaning, real combustion analysis, real safety check. A written report you can keep for warranty records.

### Easy scheduling

Quick answers
### Common questions about heating maintenance.
When should I schedule?
 [svg] 
Best window is September–October, before the first cold snap. By mid-November the schedule is packed with emergency calls and routine tune-ups get pushed. Booking early means you pick the day.
Boiler vs. furnace tune-ups — what’s different?
 [svg] 
Furnaces: combustion analysis, burner cleaning, blower service, heat exchanger inspection, gas pressure check, filter swap. Boilers: same combustion check, plus low-water cutoff test, water-side cleaning (or flush on hydronic), expansion tank, and steam-skim if applicable. Boilers usually take longer.
Carbon monoxide — how big a risk is this really?
 [svg] 
Every winter the CDC reports hundreds of CO incidents from heating equipment. Almost all of them trace back to cracked heat exchangers, blocked flues, or improper venting that an annual combustion check would have caught. It’s the single biggest safety reason to tune up combustion equipment.
What’s combustion analysis?
 [svg] 
We put a calibrated probe in the flue and measure CO, O2, CO2, flue temperature, and combustion efficiency. Numbers either pass spec or they don’t — no guesswork. Real evidence the system is burning safely and efficiently.
Combo Plan vs. single visits?
 [svg] 
If you have both AC and heating, Combo ($245) saves you $125/year vs. two singles ($185 + $185). It also gets you priority scheduling in shoulder seasons.
Steam boilers — do you service them?
 [svg] 
Yes — this is bread and butter in old Hoboken. Steam boiler tune-up includes burner work, low-water cutoff, sight-glass cleaning, skim if needed, and air-vent inspection on the radiators. We know what good steam pressure looks like.
