PROMPT 1 — REFERENCE WEBSITE: CONTENT & DATA EXTRACTION (NO DESIGN)
How to use: replace {{REFERENCE_URL}}; optionally list extra URLs under
{{EXTRA_PAGES}}. Paste the whole block below as your message.
================================================================================

Visit {{REFERENCE_URL}} and extract EVERYTHING about its CONTENT, INFORMATION
and STRUCTURE. Ignore design completely: no colors, fonts, spacing, layout
styling, animation or visual notes anywhere in the output — that is a separate
task. I want what the site SAYS, what it OFFERS, how its information is
ORGANIZED, and how it CONVERTS.

Extra pages to include: {{EXTRA_PAGES}}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD (follow in order; report which steps worked)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Fetch the homepage. Discover every internal page from: navbar links, footer
   links, in-page links, CTA destinations, and sitemap.xml / robots.txt if
   reachable. Build the page list before extracting anything.
2. Fetch every unique internal page. If there are more than 40, prioritize in
   this order: home, services/products, pricing, about/company, case studies/
   work, contact, FAQ, blog/resources index, careers, legal — and list the rest
   as "not fetched".
3. Detect JS-rendered content: if a page's server HTML is missing sections its
   nav anchors point to (e.g. #faq exists in the nav but no FAQ text was
   returned), (a) re-fetch with an alternate extraction method, (b) web-search
   the site to read indexed snippets of the missing sections, (c) record
   exactly which sections were client-rendered and only partially recovered.
   Never fill a gap with assumptions.
4. Check for a brand/press/media-kit page (footer links, /brand, /press,
   /about); capture its FACTUAL content (company facts, boilerplate, naming
   rules) — not its visual guidelines.
5. Tag every captured item with one of: [VERBATIM] (exact text) / [SUMMARY]
   (faithful paraphrase) / [INFERRED] (your conclusion — state the evidence).
   Cite the source URL for every section.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT — create a downloadable file: contentReference.txt
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Structure it EXACTLY as follows:

1. SITE OVERVIEW
   Brand name · what the company does (one line) · target audiences (each
   named with evidence) · primary conversion goal · every conversion channel
   (booking link + provider e.g. Cal.com/Calendly, forms, email, phone,
   WhatsApp, chat widget) · languages/regions · site type (one-pager, multi-
   page, app-like) · total pages found vs fetched.

2. PAGE INVENTORY
   One row per page: URL · <title> · meta description · H1 · purpose · rough
   word count · render type (server / client-side) · fetched (yes/partial/no).

3. NAVIGATION MAP
   Navbar: every link in exact order — label → destination (mark hash anchors
   vs pages), dropdown/mega-menu contents, nav CTA label → destination,
   logo destination. Mobile-only differences. Breadcrumb patterns.
   Footer: every column header + every link (label → destination), legal
   links, social platforms + URLs, newsletter presence, contact lines,
   copyright text, badges/certifications shown.

4. PAGE-BY-PAGE CONTENT (in nav order, then remaining pages)
   For each page, list each section in order:
   - Section name + purpose
   - Headline [VERBATIM] and subheadline [VERBATIM]
   - Body: faithful [SUMMARY] + word count. Verbatim excerpts of running copy
     only when the exact wording IS the data, and kept under 15 words each.
   - Lists, feature bullets, service names, tag chips, labels: [VERBATIM]
   - Every number/statistic with its unit and label: [VERBATIM]
   - CTAs: button/link label → destination
   - Forms: every field name, placeholder, type, required?, options in
     dropdowns, submit label, consent text, success/redirect behavior if
     visible
   - Media: what exists and what it depicts (photo of X / video of Y /
     3D scene / diagram) — describe only, no asset URLs, no downloads
   - Outbound links (destination + purpose)
   - Repeated/duplicated content across pages (note where it recurs)

5. OFFERINGS DATA (normalized across the whole site)
   For each product/service: name · one-liner · who it is for · features/
   scope list · deliverables · process steps with durations · timelines ·
   pricing/tiers/plans if published (exact figures, currency, billing terms,
   discounts, guarantees) · technologies/tools named · integrations ·
   SLAs/support terms · what is explicitly NOT offered.
   Also: industries served · geographies · minimum engagement · onboarding
   steps.

6. PROOF & SOCIAL PROOF
   All stats and counters · named clients/partners/logos (names only) ·
   testimonials: attribution as displayed (name, role, company) + [SUMMARY]
   of what each says (no long verbatim quotes) · case studies: client,
   problem, solution, results with numbers, services used · ratings, review
   counts and platforms · awards, certifications, press mentions, badges ·
   where on each page proof is placed relative to CTAs.

7. PEOPLE & COMPANY
   Founders/team: names + roles as displayed · founding year · headquarters
   and other locations (full addresses if shown) · hours · contact channels ·
   company size claims · careers/hiring info · culture/values statements
   [SUMMARY] · mission/vision [SUMMARY, or VERBATIM if under 15 words].

8. FAQ & OBJECTION HANDLING
   Every FAQ question [VERBATIM] + answer [SUMMARY] + which page it sits on.
   Any guarantees, risk-reversal offers, trial terms, refund policies.

9. CONTENT ASSETS
   Blog/resources: categories, post titles [VERBATIM], dates, authors,
   posting frequency · lead magnets, downloads, newsletters, webinars, tools ·
   video/podcast presence.

10. SEO & METADATA
    Title-tag pattern · meta-description pattern · OG/Twitter card presence ·
    schema.org types detected · canonical/hreflang · URL slug conventions ·
    internal-linking patterns · sitemap and robots findings · keywords the
    site visibly targets (from H1/H2 phrasing).

11. LEGAL & TRUST
    Privacy, terms, cookie policy presence + [SUMMARY] of notable clauses ·
    compliance/security claims · payment methods named · data-handling
    promises.

12. CONTENT PATTERNS & TONE
    Tone of voice (3–5 adjectives, each with one cited example) · headline
    formulas used · CTA verb patterns · typical section lengths · persuasion
    devices (scarcity badges, availability notices, social-proof placement,
    urgency) · recurring vocabulary they own · things they conspicuously
    never say or show.

13. GAPS & NOT RETRIEVABLE
    Pages/sections that could not be fetched, were client-rendered only, or
    were blocked — with the reason. Anything the site itself lacks that a
    site of its type usually has.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never invent, round, or "complete" anything. If it isn't on the site, it
  goes in section 13.
- Long-form copy gets a faithful summary, not a transcript. This file is for
  analysis and structure; their sentences are not for reuse on another site.
- No design/style/layout/animation observations of any kind in this file.
- Keep every claim traceable: URL + tag ([VERBATIM]/[SUMMARY]/[INFERRED]).
- Deliver contentReference.txt as a downloadable file, then end your reply
  with a 5-line summary of the biggest takeaways (what they sell, how they
  convert, strongest proof, structure pattern, notable gaps).
